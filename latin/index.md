# Latin words and phrases

About 350 Latin words and phrases with their meanings, from *ab initio* to *vulgo*. This was a fun project that started with a few words and then got out of hand :-)

Also available as a [PDF](../files/latinwords.pdf) and as [LaTeX source](../files/latinwords.tex).

**ab initio** – from the beginning

**ab origine** – from the origin

**ab ovo** – from the egg/beginning

**addenda** – things to be added, list of additions

**ad finem** – to the end

**ad hoc** – to or with respect to this (object); said of a body elected or appointed for a definite work

**ad libitum** – at pleasure, as much as one pleases

**ad nauseam** – to the point of disgust or satiety

**ad rem** – to the purpose; to the point

**adsum** – I am present; here!

**ad valorem** – according to the value

**advocatus diaboli** – devil's advocate; a person chosen to dispute before the papal court the claims of a candidate for canonization

**æquo animo** – with an equable mind; with equanimity

**ære perennius** – more enduring than brass or bronze

**a fortiori** – with stronger reason

**alter ego** – other self

**anguis in herba** – a snake in the grass; an unsuspected danger

**anno urbis conditæ** – in the year (or from the time) of the founded city (Rome).

**ante/post meridiem** – a.m. and p.m. (before/after noon)

**a posteriori** – from effect to cause; empirical

**a priori** – from cause to effect; presumptive

**arbiter elegantiarum** – a judge or supreme authority in matters of taste

**arcanum imperii** – state secrets

**argumentum ad hominem** – an argument to the individual man, *i.e.*, to his interest and prejudices

**ars est celare artem** – it is true art to conceal art

**ars longa, vita brevis** – art is long, life is short

**bis dat qui cito dat** – he gives twice who gives quickly

**bona fide(s)** – good faith (in good faith)

**carpe diem** – enjoy the present day; improve the time

**casus belli** – that which causes or justifies war

**caveat emptor** – let the purchaser beware (he buys at his own risk)

**ceteris paribus** – other things being equal

**circa** – about

**cogito, ergo sum** – I think, therefore I am

**coram populo** – publicly; in public

**corpus delicti** – the body of the crime

**corrigenda** – things to be corrected; a list of errors

**credat Judæus Apella** – let Apella, the superstitious Jew, believe it; I won't

**credo quia absurdum** – I believe because it is absurd, or contrary to reason

**cum grano salis** – with a grain of salt; with some allowance

**de facto** – in point of fact; actual or actually

**de gustibus non est disputandum** – there is no disputing about tastes

**Dei gratia** – by the grace of God

**de jure** – from the law; by right

**delenda est Carthago** – Carthage must be destroyed

**de mortuis nil nisi bonum** – (say) nothing but the good of the dead

**de novo** – anew

**Deo gratias** – thanks to God

**de profundis** – out of the depths

**deus ex machina** – a god from a machine; used in reference to forced or unlikely events introduced in a drama, novel, *etc.*, to resolve a difficult or awkward situation; derived from the use of deities in the ancient drama

**dies iræ** – day of wrath

**Dominus vobiscum** – God be with you

**dramatis personæ** – characters of the drama or play

**dulce et decorum est pro patria mori** – it is sweet and glorious to die for one's country

**dum spiro, spero** – while I breathe, I hope

**dum vivimus, vivamus** – while we live, let us live

**ecce homo** – behold the man!

**editio princeps** – the first printed edition of a book

**ego et rex meus** – I and my king

**emeritus** – retired or superannuated after long service

**e pluribus unum** – one out of many; one composed of many (motto of the U.S.)

**errata** – errors; list of errors

**est modus in rebus** – there is a medium in all things

**et alia (et al.)** – and others; and elsewhere

**et cætera** or **et cetera** – and the rest

**et id genus omne** – and everything of the sort

**et tu, Brute!** – and you also, Brutus!

**ex cathedra** – from the chair; with high authority

**excelsior** – higher, taller, loftier

**exempli gratia (e.g.)** – for example; such as

**exeunt omnes** – all go out (or retire)

**exit** – he goes out

**ex nihilo nihil fit** – out of nothing, nothing comes

**ex officio** – in virtue of (his) office

**ex parte** – from one party or side

**ex pede Herculem** – from the foot we recognize Hercules; we judge the whole from the part

**experto crede** – trust one who has experience

**ex post facto** – after the deed is done; retrospective

**ex situ** – opposite of **in situ**

**ex uno disce omnes** – from one judge of the rest

**facile princeps** – easily preeminent; indisputably the first

**facilis est descensus Averni** – the descent to Avernus (hell) is easy

**festina lente** – hasten slowly

**fiat justitia, ruat cœlum** – let justice be done though the heavens should fall

**fiat lux** – let there be light

**fides Punica** – Punic (or Carthaginian) faith; treachery

**fidus Achates** – faithful Achates (a true friend)

**finis coronat opus** – the end crowns the work

**flagrante delicto** – in the commission of the crime; red-handed

**fons et origo** – the source and origin

**fortiter in re** – with firmness in acting

**fortuna favet fortibus** – fortune favors the bold

**furor loquendi** – a rage for speaking

**furor scribendi** – a rage for writing

**gaudeamus igitur** – so let us be joyful

**genius loci** – the genius (or guarding spirit) of the place

**hic et ubique** – here and everywhere

**hic jacet** – here lies

**hinc illæ lacrimæ** – hence these tears

**hodie mihi, cras tibi** – mine today, yours tomorrow

**homo sum; humani nihil a me alienum puto** – I am a man, I count nothing human indifferent to me

**horribile dictu** – horrible to relate

**humanum est errare** – to err is human

**idem** – same as previously given

**ibidem** – at the same place (in a book)

**id est (i.e.)** – that is

**ignotum per ignotius** – the unknown (explained) by the still more unknown

**impedimenta** – encumbrances, luggage, baggage

**in æternum** – forever

**in articulo mortis** – at the point of death; in the last struggle

**index expurgatorius** – a list of prohibited works

**in esse** – in being, in actuality

**in extenso** – at full length

**in extremis** – at the point of death

**infra dignitatem** – below one's dignity

**in loco** – in the place; in the natural (or proper) place

**in loco parentis** – in place of the parent

**in medias res** – into the midst of things

**in memoriam** – to the memory of, in memory

**in nomine** – in the name of

**in omnia paratus** – prepared for all things

**in perpetuum** – forever

**in posse** – in possible existence; in possibility

**in præsenti** – at the present moment

**in propria persona** – in one's own person

**in puris naturalibus** – quite naked

**in re** – in the matter of

**in rerum natura** – in the nature of things

**in sæcula sæculorum** – for ages on ages

**in situ** – in its original position, undisturbed; in a localized state or condition

**in statu quo** – in the former state

**inter alia** – among other things

**inter nos** – between ourselves

**in terrorem** – as a warning

**in toto** – in the whole; entirely

**intra muros** – within the walls

**in transitu** – in the course of transit

**in vacuo** – in the empty space; in a vacuum

**in vino veritas** – there is truth in wine

**invita Minerva** – against the will of Minerva (Athena); without genius or natural abilities

**ipse dixit** – he himself said it; a dogmatic saying or assertion

**ipsissima verba** – the very words

**ipso facto** – by that very fact

**ipso jure** – by the law itself

**jure divino** – by divine law

**jure humano** – by human law

**laborare est orare** – to labor is to pray; work is worship

**labor omnia vincit** – labor conquers everything

**lapsus calami** – slip of the pen

**lapsus linguæ** – slip of the tongue

**lapsus memoriæ** – slip of the memory

**lares et penates** – household gods

**laudator temporis acti** – a praiser of past times

**laus Deo** – praise to God

**lex non scripta** – unwritten law; common law

**lex scripta** – statute law

**littera scripta manet** – the written word remains

**locum tenens** – one occupying the place of another; substitute

**longo intervallo** – by or at a long interval

**lucus a non lucendo** – used as typical of an absurd derivation: lucus, a grove, having been derived by an old grammarian from luceo, to shine; "from not shining"

**lusus naturæ** – a sport or freak of nature

**magna est veritas, et prævalebit** – truth is mighty, and will prevail

**magnum opus** – a great work

**mala fides** – with bad faith; treacherously

**mare clausum** – a closed sea; a sea belonging to a single nation

**materfamilias** – mother of a family

**mea culpa** – my fault; by my fault

**me judice** – I being judge; in my opinion

**memento mori** – remember that you must die; a reminder of death

**mens sana in corpore sano** – a sound mind in a sound body

**mens sibi conscia recti** – a mind conscious of rectitude

**meo periculo** – at my own risk

**meum et tuum** – mine and yours

**mirabile dictu** – wonderful to relate

**mirabile visu** – wonderful to see

**modus operandi** – manner of working

**modus vivendi** – manner of living; used of a temporary working agreement or compromise

**monumentum ære perennius** – a monument more lasting than brass

**more majorum** – after the manner of our ancestors

**morituri te salutamus** – we about to die salute you; said by the Roman gladiators to the emperor

**motu proprio** – of his own accord

**multum in parvo** – much in little

**mutatis mutandis** – with the necessary changes

**natura non facit saltum** – nature does not make a leap

**nemine contradicente** – no one speaking in opposition; unopposed

**nemine dissentiente** – no one dissenting

**nemo me impune lacessit** – no one assails me with impunity (motto of Scotland)

**ne plus ultra** – nothing further; the uttermost point; perfection

**ne quid nimis** – avoid excess

**nil admirari** – to be astonished at nothing

**nil desperandum** – there is no reason for despair

**nolens volens** – unwilling or willing

**noli me tangere** – touch me not

**non compos mentis** – not of sound mind

**non est** – he or it is not

**non est inventus** – he has not been found

**non libet** – it does not please (me)

**non liquet** – the case is not clear

**non multa, sed multum** – not many things, but much

**non nobis solum** – not for ourselves alone

**non omnis moriar** – I shall not wholly die

**non sequitur** – it does not follow

**nosce te ipsum** – know thyself

**nota bene** – note well; take notice

**novus homo** – a new man; one who has raised himself from obscurity

**nulla dies sine linea** – not a day without a line; no day without something done

**nunc aut nunquam** – now or never

**obiit** – he or she died

**obiter dictum** – a thing said by the way

**odi profanum vulgus** – I loathe the profane rabble

**odium theologicum** – the hatred of theologians

**omnia vincit amor** – love conquers all things

**onus probandi** – the burden of proof

**operæ pretium est** – it is worth while

**opere citato (op. cit.)** – in the work cited (means you don't know the page number!)

**ora et labora** – pray and work

**ora pro nobis** – pray for us

**ore rotundo** – with round full voice; well-turned speech

**O! si sic omnia** – O, if all things (were) so; O, if he had always so spoken or acted

**O tempora! O mores!** – Alas for the times! Alas for the manners (or morals)!

**otium cum dignitate** – ease with dignity

**pace** – by leave of; not to give offense to

**palmam qui meruit ferat** – let him who has won the palm wear it

**pari passu** – with equal pace; side by side

**par nobile fratrum** – a noble pair of brothers; two just alike

**particeps criminis** – an accomplice in a crime

**passim** – everywhere; throughout; in all parts of the book, chapter, etc.

**paterfamilias** – father of a family; head of a household

**pater patriæ** – father of his country

**pax vobiscum** – peace be with you

**peccavi** – I have sinned (or been to blame)

**per** – for; through; by

**per annum** – by the year; annually

**per capita** – by heads; for each individual

**per centum** – by the hundred

**per contra** – on the contrary

**per diem** – by the day; daily

**per fas et nefas** – through right and wrong

**per se** – by itself

**persona non grata** – an unacceptable person

**plebs** – the common people

**pons asinorum** – the asses' bridge; a name for the fifth proposition of the first book of Euclid

**post hoc ergo propter hoc** – after this, therefore, on account of this; subsequent to, therefore due to this: an illogical way of reasoning; believing that temporal succession implies a causal relation

**prima facie** – at first view or consideration

**primum mobile** – the source of motion; the mainspring

**principia, non homines** – principles, not men

**pro bono publico** – for the good of the public

**pro et contra** – for and against

**profanum vulgus** – the profane herd

**pro forma** – for the sake of form

**pro patria** – for our country

**pro rata** – according to rate or proportion

**pro tanto** – for so much; as far as it goes

**Punica fides** – Punic or Carthaginian faith; treachery

**qualis rex, talis grex** – like king, like people

**quantum libet** – as much as you please

**quantum sufficit** – as much as suffices

**quid pro quo** – something in return; an equivalent

**quis custodiet ipsos custodes?** – who shall guard the guards themselves?

**quoad hoc** – to this extent

**quoad sacra** – as far as sacred things are concerned; for ecclesiastical purposes only

**quos Deus vult perdere, prius dementat** – those whom God wishes to destroy, he first makes mad

**quod erat demonstrandum** – which was to be proved or demonstrated

**quod vide** – which see

**quorum pars magna fui** – of which things, I was an important part

**quot homines, tot sententiæ** – many men, many minds

**rara avis** – a rare bird, a paragon

**reductio ad absurdum** – a reducing to the absurd; a method of proof where a proposition is shown to be true by demonstrating the absurdity of its contradiction

**requiescat in pace** – may he rest in peace

**res angusta domi** – narrow circumstances at home; poverty

**res gestæ** – things done; exploits; history

**respice finem** – look to the end

**resurgam** – I shall rise again

**rus in urbe** – the country in town

**sanctum sanctorum** – holy of holies

**sartor resartus** – the patcher repatched; the tailor patched or mended

**satis superque** – enough, and more than enough

**satis verborum** – enough of words; no more need to be said

**scripsit** – wrote (it)

**sculpsit** – engraved (it)

**secundum artem** – according to the art or rule

**semper fidelis** – always faithful

**semper idem** – always the same

**seriatim** – in a series; one by one

**sic** – thus, so

**sic itur ad astra** – such is the way to the stars or immortality

**sic passim** – so here and there throughout; so everywhere

**sic transit gloria mundi** – thus passes away the glory of this world

**sicut ante** – as before

**similia similibus curantur** – like things are cured by like

**simplex munditiis** – elegant in simplicity

**sine cura** – without charge or care

**sine die** – without a day being appointed

**sine qua non** – without which; something indispensable

**siste, viator** – stop, traveler

**sit tibi terra levis** – light lie the earth upon thee

**spero meliora** – I hope for better things

**splendide mendax** – nobly untruthful; untrue for a good object

**sponte sua** – of one's (or its) own accord

**status quo** – the state in which; the existing condition

**stet** – let it stand; do not delete

**suaviter in modo, fortiter in re** – gentle in manner, resolute in execution

**sub judice** – under consideration

**sub rosa** – under the rose; confidentially

**sui generis** – of its own peculiar kind; in a class by itself

**summum bonum** – the chief good

**sunt lacrimæ rerum** – there are tears for things; misfortunes call for tears

**suppressio veri** – a suppression of the truth

**sursum corda** – lift up your hearts

**suum cuique** – let every one have his own

**tabula rasa** – a smooth or blank tablet

**te judice** – you being the judge

**tempus fugit** – time flies

**terminus ad quem** – the term (or limit) to which

**terminus a quo** – the term (or limit) from which

**terra firma** – solid earth; a secure foothold

**terra incognita** – an unknown country

**tertium quid** – a third something; a nondescript

**timeo Danaos et dona ferentes** – I fear the Greeks, even when they bring gifts

**tot homines, quot sententiæ** – so many men, so many minds

**toto cælo** – by the whole heavens; diametrically opposite

**tu quoque** – you also

**ubi supra** – where above mentioned

**ultima Thule** – most distant Thule; utmost limit

**urbi et orbi** – to the city and to the world

**utile dulci** – the useful with the agreeable

**ut infra** – as below

**ut supra** – as above

**væ victis** – woe to the vanquished

**vale** – farewell

**varium et mutabile semper femina** – woman is ever a changeful and capricious thing

**veni, vidi, vici** – I came, I saw, I conquered (Cæsar's message to the Senate when he conquered Pharnaces, king of Pontus)

**verbatim et literatim** – word for word and letter for letter

**verbum sat sapienti** – a word is enough for a wise man

**via, veritas, vita** – the way, the truth, the life

**vice versa** – the terms of the case being interchanged or reversed; conversely

**videlicet (viz.)** – that is to say, namely (literally: one may see)

**vide ut supra** – see what is stated above

**vi et armis** – by force and arms; by main force

**vincit qui se vincit** – he conquers who conquers himself

**virginibus puerisque** – for maidens and boys

**vis a tergo** – a force from behind

**vis inertiæ** – the power of inertia; resistance to force applied

**vis medicatrix naturæ** – the healing power of nature

**vis vitæ** – living force; energy

**vivat regina (rex)!** – long live the queen (king)!

**viva voce** – by the living voice; orally

**vox et præterea nihil** – a voice and nothing more; sound but no sense

**vox populi, vox Dei** – the voice of the people is the voice of God
